Projects

  1. Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models
    Research Project Sep. 2024 — Present

    Background: (1) Multi-turn retrieval-based conditional story generation ignores inter-evidence dependencies, often sufferring from logical inconsistency and topic drift; (2) Existing emotional support dialogue systems focus only on single-turn emotion matching and overlook the trajectory of emotional shifts across multiple turns.

    Contributions: (1) Built a key–value retrieval repository of "condition / content" pairs and proposed an MCTS-based multi-turn evidence-planning framework that leverages a logic discriminator to search for optimal evidence paths in the evidence tree, balancing topic controllability and plot coherence. (2) Proposed an emotion-trajectory retrieval-augmented Markov emotion transition model to track dynamic emotional shifts in multi-turn dialogue, combined with Q-learning to adaptively select user-specific emotional support strategies.

    Outcomes: Published as first-author at TASLP (CCF B) and ACL Findings 2026 (CCF A).

  2. LLM Safety
    Research Project Dec. 2023 — Apr. 2025

    Background: Current LLM jailbreak defenses mostly rely on direct refusal, which can be easily identified and exploited by attackers to strengthen future attacks.

    Contributions: The first work to defend against jailbreak attacks by disguising the defensive intent. Proposed an LLM-based multi-stage multi-agent game framework in which an attacker and a disguiser iteratively select in-context examples and strategies via adversarial reinforcement learning, converging toward a Nash equilibrium. The disguiser ultimately produces responses that are safe and relevant to the attack query while hiding the defense intent and revealing no harmful information.

    Outcomes: Published as student-second-author in TIFS (CCF A).

  3. Text Error Correction
    Research Project Dec. 2022 — Jun. 2024

    Background: (1)Chinese semantic errors are subtle and hard to annotate, with limited labeled data; Existing syntax-augmented models require training from scratch at high cost; (2) Existing spelling correction models do not fully exploit the discriminative signals between phonologically and visually confusable characters.

    Contributions: (1) Proposed a pseudo-label data construction method for Chinese semantic error recognition, along with a method that fuses dependency-syntax information into pre-trained models only during fine-tuning. (2) Proposed reverse contrastive learning that maximizes the distance between anchor characters and easily-confusable negative samples.

    Outcomes: Published as first-author at COLING 2025 (CCF B) and student-first-author at JCST (CCF B).

Work Experience

  • Applied Research Intern, Tencent WXG May.2026 — Present

    Led the end-to-end design and implementation of "People Also Ask (大家都在问)", an LLM-powered QA module on product detail pages that surfaces high-frequency user questions with sourced answers.

    • Phase 1 — QA Mining: Built an SOP pipeline over ~140k products and 6 months of CS dialogues. Used LLM-enhanced embeddings for DBSCAN clustering, with intent-label hard-cluster fallback to identify recurring questions, paired with source-grounded and ABSA-based review mining for answers. Answer correctness: 53.22% → 93.23% (+35.82pp); per-product recall: 3.45 → 13.33 questions (+286%); ≥3-QA coverage: 50% → 99%.
    • Cyclic Update: Designed a monthly LLM-driven pipeline for question deduplication and fact-conflict verification, enabling safe minimum-diff incremental updates.
    • Phase 2 — Cold-Start Expansion: Built a few-shot generative pipeline via L4-category QA migration, expanding coverage from 5.7% → ~68% (~100k products, ~10× lift), with an automated evaluation framework to guide category rollout.
    • Model Consolidation: Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct on pipeline outputs to replace the hand-crafted SOP with a lightweight, maintainable model-based service.
  • NLP Intern, Whale Cloud. Apr. 2024 – Aug. 2024

    Targeted spelling errors in mobile ChatBI user inputs in vertical domains.

    • Collected 10,022 human-annotated samples and 175,151 auto-generated samples; comparative analysis revealed significant differences from SIGHAN in text formality, input method, and user population.
    • Introduced a bi-directional KL-divergence constraint during training and an iterative inference strategy to mitigate over-correction, achieving approximately 1.0%–4.9% gains on both the in-house dataset and SIGHAN benchmarks.
